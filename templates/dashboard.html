Guide

What this project is, the five pipeline steps, and the words used on every other tab.

What is this?

A prediction market is a site where people trade shares on a yes/no question. A YES price of 0.30 means the crowd thinks there is a 30% chance. That price is the market's forecast.

Edgebook asks a forecaster called Jev for its own chance. When Jev disagrees with the crowd enough, the desk places a paper bet (fake money, never real). Once the market's real answer is known, the desk scores who was closer to the truth.

How it works: five steps, always in this order

  1. 1

    Load markets

    Copies the questions into the local database, each with the YES price taken before the answer was known.

  2. 2

    Gather news

    Saves headlines published before that price date. Jev may read them. Nothing from after the date is allowed in.

  3. 3

    Ask Jev

    Writes down one chance (0 to 100%) per market, using the Context chosen in the top bar: basic is the question only, news adds headlines.

  4. 4

    Place paper bets

    Bets only where Jev and the market disagree by enough, and Jev is confident and well informed. A bigger gap means a bigger stake, up to a cap.

  5. 5

    Settle and score

    Looks up the real answer, marks each bet won or lost, and scores Jev against the market.

Glossary and worked example

Market price
The crowd's chance of YES, from 0 to 1. A price of 0.30 means 30%.
Jev probability
The forecaster's own chance of YES for the same question, written down before the answer is known.
Edge
Jev probability minus market price. Example: Jev 0.40, market 0.30, edge +0.10. The desk only bets when the edge is large enough (default 0.08), and Jev is confident (0.6) and informed (0.4) enough.
Paper bet
A pretend bet. Edge above zero buys YES at the market price, below zero buys NO at 1 minus the price. Stake grows with the edge and is capped.
Settle
Once the real answer is known, each bet is marked won or lost and its profit or loss is worked out.
Brier score
Average of (forecast minus outcome) squared. Example: Jev says 0.80 and the market resolves YES (1). Miss = (0.80 - 1)2 = 0.04. If it had resolved NO (0) the miss would be (0.80 - 0)2 = 0.64. Confident and wrong hurts most.
Calibration
Do the percentages mean what they say? Of all the times 70% was forecast, YES should happen about 70% of the time.
Stub vs Jev
The stub is an offline placeholder that takes the market price and nudges it by a fixed amount of up to 10 points, worked out from the market id. It lets the whole pipeline run without keys, so it shows the machinery working, not forecasting skill. Jev is the real API, used only when a key is configured.
Basic vs news
The context given to the decider. Basic is the question, rules, category and dates. News adds headlines published before the price date, never after.
